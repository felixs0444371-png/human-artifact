Algorithms can be useful: they help us find creators, communities, and games we actually enjoy. The problem is that convenience can hide the trade. Our attention and personal data help decide what gets amplified. Targeted advertising also rewards platforms for learning as much as possible about us.

THE BIGGER EFFECT

What starts as entertainment can shape...

01

Our time

Autoplay and an endless feed remove the natural moment when we would normally decide to stop.

02

Our view

If the system keeps showing what already holds our attention, different ideas become easier to miss.

03

Our choices

Repeated recommendations can make a product, opinion, or trend feel more popular or more inevitable than it really is.

THE EXTREME OUTCOME

If this keeps getting more personalized, the future may not look like technology forcing us to do something. It may look like technology becoming so good at predicting us that we stop noticing when a suggestion becomes a decision.

TAKE BACK THE CONTROLLER

Make the feed work for you.

We do not have to delete every app or stop enjoying technology. A few intentional moves can put some friction back into the loop.

  1. 01
    Choose before you open.

    Know what you came to watch instead of letting the homepage decide.

  2. 02
    Train it on purpose.

    Use “Not interested,” remove accidental watches, and pause history when researching.

  3. 03
    Build a stopping point.

    Turn off autoplay or decide “one match, one video” before starting.

  4. 04
    Step outside the feed.

    Use subscriptions, direct search, and sources that do not already match your usual view.
