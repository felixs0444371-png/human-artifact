Recommendation Algorithms and My Feed

How platforms like YouTube learn what I like and use that information to choose what I see next.

Main idea: My feed is personalized, but it is not neutral. Every video I watch, search, like, or skip gives the platform information about what might keep my attention.

Why this matters right now

97%of U.S. teens use the internet every day
3 in 4teens use YouTube daily
85%of teens play video games

How recommendations work

1

I do something

I watch, search, like, skip, or click a video.

2

The platform collects signals

It learns from my watch history and compares my activity with similar users.

3

The system ranks videos

It predicts which videos I am most likely to click and enjoy.

4

My feed changes

I receive more similar content, which gives the system even more information.

My personal example

I see this with soccer, gaming, and EA FC videos. If I watch one video after a match, my homepage can quickly fill with highlights, tactics, pack openings, and reactions. I chose the first video, but the algorithm helps choose what I see after that.

Why I think it is important

  • Time: Autoplay and endless recommendations make it easy to keep watching longer than I planned.
  • Information: Seeing similar content repeatedly can make other ideas easier to miss.
  • Choices: Repeated recommendations can make a product, trend, or opinion look more popular than it really is.
  • Privacy: Platforms collect information about our activity to improve recommendations and advertising.

What I can do

Search for what I actually want instead of only using the homepage.
Use the Not interested option when a recommendation does not fit me.
Turn off autoplay and decide when I want to stop watching.
Look at sources and opinions outside of my normal feed.
